# Detecting confusion: signals and types

Contents: 1. Evidence levels · 2. Explicit signals · 3. Implicit signals · 4. Ten confusion types · 5. Confusion vs. look-alikes · 6. False positives · 7. Confidence and response

## 1. Evidence levels

Treat every signal as a clue to investigate, not proof.

| Level | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear** | The user says it, or two or more independent signals agree | Address the difficulty directly |
| **Possible** | One implicit signal, or an ambiguous message | Help under your best reading, state the assumption, ask at most one question |
| **None** | Only weak signals (short, informal, typos, basic question) | Answer the request. Do not mention confusion |

## 2. Explicit signals

The user directly says they are lost, unsure, or stuck. They appear in any language and register. Examples:

- English: "I don't get it", "I'm lost", "this makes no sense", "what does this even mean?", "I have no idea where to start", "I'm stuck", "wait, what?", "I can't figure out why"
- Indonesian: "bingung", "gak ngerti", "gatau harus mulai dari mana", "maksudnya apa sih", "kok bisa gitu", "kok masih error", "mentok"
- Also: "can you explain that differently?", "I read it three times", "I've tried everything"

Explicit does not always mean confusion about content. "I'm lost" can mean the task, the goal, or the interface. Find out which before explaining.

## 3. Implicit signals

Look across the conversation, not just the last message.

- **Repetition**: the same question reappears in different words after an answer.
- **Wrong restatement**: they paraphrase your explanation and get it subtly wrong. Their error shows the exact gap.
- **Applied incorrectly**: they follow your instruction but at the wrong place or in the wrong order.
- **Answering a different question**: their reply addresses something other than what you asked.
- **Contradictory requirements**: "make it short but include everything", or two goals that conflict, with no sign they notice.
- **Goal-less request**: "help me with my project" with nothing else.
- **Option overload**: a list of choices plus "I don't know which".
- **Task stalling**: long gaps between attempts in the same spot, repeated restarts, "let me start over".
- **Surface fixation**: they ask about a symptom ("why is this red?") when the cause is upstream.
- **Terminology drift**: they use a term incorrectly in a way that suggests a wrong mental model.

## 4. Ten confusion types

Each type points to a different default move.

| # | Type | Typical signals | Default approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Unclear thinking** | Rambling, many half-formed points, "my thoughts are all over the place" | Step-by-step: pull out the single main point first, then order the rest |
| 2 | **No ideas** | "I have nothing", blank page, "any ideas?" with no constraints | Example: show 2 to 3 concrete angles, ask which one resonates |
| 3 | **Hard to organize or express** | Pastes a draft, "it feels messy", can't say what they mean | Step-by-step: purpose, then structure, then wording |
| 4 | **Not understanding information or instructions** | Quotes a sentence, "what does this mean", misread steps | Explanation: plain restatement, then the reason |
| 5 | **Cannot decide** | "A or B?", loops between options, asks for more and more info | Narrow to the 2 criteria that matter, give a lean |
| 6 | **No clear start, continuation, or finish** | "Where do I begin?", "what next?", "how do I finish this?" | Step-by-step: one next action with a done-check |
| 7 | **Not understanding an error or failure** | Pastes an error, "why didn't it work?", "it worked before" | Explanation of what the message means, then the smallest next test |
| 8 | **Misreading a situation** | Reads a message, event, or outcome in a way the facts do not support | Gently surface the facts, offer another plausible reading, do not overrule feelings |
| 9 | **Communication gap** | "What did they mean by that?", "how do I say this?" | Explanation of likely intents, plus a clarifying question they could ask the other person |
| 10 | **Sudden change** | "Everything changed", plans broke, new constraints | Step-by-step: what is fixed, what changed, what is the next safe step |

Types often overlap. A person facing a sudden change (10) may also be unable to decide (5). Start with the one blocking progress right now.

## 5. Confusion vs. look-alikes

| It might be... | How it differs | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity** | They want depth, not rescue. Tone is exploratory ("why does X work that way?") | Give a good explanation. No remedial framing |
| **Disagreement** | They understood and reject it. They give counter-arguments | Engage with the argument. Do not re-explain as if they missed it |
| **Impatience** | They want the answer fast. Often terse | Lead with the answer, trim everything else |
| **Missing information** | They know what they want but lack a fact | Provide the fact, or ask for the missing input |
| **Testing you** | They probe edge cases or trick questions | Answer directly and carefully |
| **Expertise gap, not confusion** | They know the domain, use different terms | Map terms to theirs, do not simplify |
| **Distress** | The difficulty is emotional more than informational | Respond to the person first. See SKILL.md Boundaries |

## 6. False positives

None of these alone establishes confusion:

- A short message, slang, lowercase, emoji, or chat-style writing
- Typos or grammar mistakes, including non-native writing
- A basic or beginner question
- Asking for more detail or a source
- Disagreeing or stating a preference
- Going quiet after an answer
- Asking a follow-up question (that is usually progress)

If this is all you have, treat the message as a normal request.

## 7. Confidence and response

Phrase your response to match your confidence.

- **High**: "The part that usually trips people up here is X."
- **Medium**: "It sounds like the sticking point might be X. Tell me if I'm off."
- **Low**: Answer normally. If you must ask, one concrete question: "Is the problem the setup or the result?"

Avoid generic probes like "What exactly are you confused about?". They push the diagnostic work back to the person who is already struggling.
